        SSS001
        Aircraft shall not occupy the same runway protected volume
        While an aircraft occupies a runway protected volume, the system shall refuse every other clearance that would place a second aircraft in that volume or in a volume configured as intersecting or dependent. Independent parallel runways are separate volumes. Acceptance is two aircraft and one runway where the second line-up, landing, or takeoff is refused until the first aircraft is airborne or clear, and the same refusal across a configured conflict pair.
        Loss of runway separation is the catastrophic condition this increment exists to prevent.

        SSS002
        Each airport shall run on n ATC computers for integer n greater than 0
        Each airport shall be configured with an integer computer count n greater than 0 and shall start a local clearance group of that size. A configuration of one computer is a valid rural airport and shall still apply the separation guard. Acceptance is a startup with n equal to 1 that serves local traffic, and a startup that rejects n less than 1.
        Redundancy is configured per airport. One computer must remain a legal and safe configuration.

        SSS003
        Airports inside communications distance shall exchange the state a handoff requires
        An airport shall treat another airport as a neighbor only when the distance between their configured positions is within the configured communications distance, and it shall exchange handoff requests with such a neighbor. Airports farther apart shall not be offered a handoff. Acceptance is three airports where only the pair inside the distance can complete a handoff.
        Communications distance is the policy that decides who is in the cluster.

        SSS004
        Loss of the outside link shall fail smart and preserve local separation
        When an airport can no longer exchange traffic state with a neighbor inside the configured communications distance, the local computers shall keep runway assignment, sequencing, landing, and takeoff service for aircraft already inside that airport's protected volume. The airport shall not issue a handoff or any other clearance that depends on neighbor state it cannot confirm. Last-known neighbor data shall stay marked stale and shall not reduce local separation. Coordinated handoffs shall resume only after the neighbor link is revalidated. This shall hold for a rural airport with one computer and no reachable neighbor.
        Fail smart means the local tower continues and unconfirmed neighbor state cannot make a local clearance look safer.

        The tower shall give runway assignment, sequence, landing, takeoff, hold short, line up and wait, and go-around
        The tower shall offer runway assignment, sequence position, hold short, line up and wait, takeoff clearance, landing clearance, and go-around, and no other clearance kind. Each offer shall name the aircraft and the runway, and a sequenced aircraft shall also receive its position. Acceptance is one aircraft that can be assigned, queued, held short, lined up, cleared for takeoff or landing, and sent around, with any other kind refused.
        Hold short, line up and wait, and go-around are required to keep the runway exclusive. The set is the tower subset of FAA JO 7110.65 used here.

        SSS006
        Runways and their designations shall come from one TOML file per airport
        Each airport shall be configured by one TOML file that names the airport, its position, communications distance, computer count, envelope bounds, and every runway. Each runway shall have an identifier, a designation of arrival, departure, both, or closed, and a flag stating whether it is independent of the other runways. The system shall not control traffic from a missing or invalid file. Acceptance is a file edit that closes a runway and a following assignment to that runway being refused.
        The airport is meant to be retargeted by editing data, not by editing the clearance code.

        SSS007
        Separation shall be demonstrated by a TLA+ model under stated assumptions and bounds
        The separation invariant and the admissible clearance transitions shall be specified in TLA+ and checked with TLC on a finite model of at most 4 runways, 8 aircraft, and 2 neighbor airports. Every admissible transition in that model shall preserve exclusive protected volume, dependent-runway exclusion, and queue order, and shall refuse a neighbor-dependent clearance while the neighbor link is down. The software shall refuse to control a traffic picture outside that envelope. Acceptance is a TLC run that reports success on the checked model and a startup rejection above the envelope.
        A finite model check is the formal evidence this increment can actually produce. Operation outside it is refused rather than implied to be proved.

        SSS008
        The Erlang service shall be tied to that model by a refinement argument
        The Erlang clearance state shall be related to the TLA+ state by a written refinement mapping that names the corresponding variables and the assumptions it depends on, including the single ordered log. PropEr state-machine tests shall sample the Erlang transitions, including concurrent requests, process failure, recovery, and replica agreement, and those tests shall be recorded as conformance evidence rather than as a proof. Any obligation the mapping does not discharge shall stay labeled open. Acceptance is a mapping that a reader can check against the module and the spec, plus a test run that fails the build when a sampled transition breaks the invariant.
        Testing does not establish implementation correctness. The mapping is the separate argument that connects the two descriptions.

        SSS009
        When n is greater than 1, one local computer failure shall leave a single clearance authority
        When more than one computer is configured, clearances shall be appended to one ordered log and only a majority member that is the elected authority may append. After one computer fails, a surviving majority shall keep exactly one authority and the failed computer shall not issue clearances. For two computers the majority is both, so one failure stops clearances instead of leaving two authorities. Acceptance is a group of three where killing one leaves one authority, and a partition into one and two where only the side of two issues a clearance.
        The safety claim is a single authority. Two computers cannot survive a failure without risking a split brain, so they stop.

        SSS010
        A clearance the separation guard rejects shall not be issued
        The system shall apply the separation guard before it commits any clearance that changes runway occupancy, and it shall not put a rejected clearance on the ordered log as an issued clearance. The pilot-facing answer to a rejected request shall be a refusal that names the aircraft and the reason class. Acceptance is a request that would double-occupy a runway, with the log unchanged and the caller receiving a refusal.
        The guard is useful only if a rejected transition never becomes the issued history.
